A smart contract can accept a valid zero-knowledge (ZK) proof yet execute an action which may not be authorized by the proved statement, resulting in duplicate asset claims, misdirected token issuance, or loss of credential integrity. Binding the proved statement to the contract's action requires agreement among the circuit, generated verifier, and consuming contract. Analyzing the circuit or contract alone cannot establish that agreement. To study circuit–contract binding in Circom/Solidity applications, we derive a taxonomy of binding flaws from audits, incidents, and vulnerability datasets and build a version-pinned benchmark pairing vulnerable and repaired applications with adversarial synthetic cases. Guided by this taxonomy, we design BindCheck, a cross-layer checker that traces supported contract effects through the verifier's public inputs to the circuit's public interface and examines the verifier's configuration. Our evaluation combines analysis of a preregistered sample of open-source projects, an on-chain verifier census, and local execution-based validation. We identify Groth16 verifier keys that admit forged proofs for arbitrary admissible public inputs and find matching weak-key patterns in deployed verifier contracts. In separate application case studies, we also uncover binding flaws, previously unreported to our knowledge, in which valid proofs are accepted while contracts act on recipients or credentials that the proved statement does not determine. We reproduce these flaws on local chains and validate scoped repairs that reject the demonstrated attacks while preserving legitimate executions.
