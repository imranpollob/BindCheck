\section{Motivating Example: Aztec Connect}\label{sec:motivating}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\input{figures/f0-aztec-extent}
\caption{Aztec Connect. The public-input hash covers all $L$ rows of a chunk;
the settlement loop discharges deposits only for rows below $k$, read from
unhashed calldata. A non-empty row at or beyond $k$ updates the proved rollup
state, but its deposit is never collected (extent binding, direction~(b)).}
\label{fig:aztec}
\end{figure}

In June 2026 the deprecated, immutable Aztec Connect rollup contract lost
about \usdM{\AztecLossUSD} without any cryptographic
failure~\cite{aztec-incident,aztec-blocksec,aztec-rekt}. Each rollup proof
committed, through a hash in its public input, to every row of a fixed-size
chunk of transactions (Fig.~\ref{fig:aztec}). The settlement loop processed
only the first $k$ rows, and $k$ came from calldata outside the hash. A row
inside the chunk but past $k$ updated the proved rollup state, crediting a
deposit, while the matching on-chain obligation, collecting the deposited
funds, never ran. Withdrawals then paid out against the unbacked credit. The
verifier was sound and the contract checked the proof; they disagreed about
which rows existed. We rebuilt this shape with a real Groth16 verifier on a
local chain: an accepted proof left \AztecTestbedUnbackedGap{} token units
credited but unbacked, and neither Slither nor our checker's earlier freeze
flagged it.%
