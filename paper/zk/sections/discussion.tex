\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\para{What fail-closed buys, and what it costs.} On paired cases, \tool{}
issued fewer warnings on legitimate cases than either composed baseline and
certified no vulnerable case, at the cost of deciding 15 of 46 cases; on DEV
it decided 0 of 27 applicable rows for P2.%
% src: reports/redteam/REPORT.md#what-the-comparison-supports; paper/zk/data/redteam-arms.json; paper/zk/data/frozen-dev-stats.json#applicability.P2
\
An analyst who receives \UNK{} with a reason code knows what to inspect; an
analyst who receives a false ``safe'' does not look. We consider that trade
correct for certification, but it means \tool{} is a triage instrument, not a
replacement for review.

\para{Detection does not need the circuit; certification does.} Every frozen
violation is decidable from the consumer and verifier, so a purpose-built
contract-only rule reaches the same warnings
(\S\ref{sec:res-ablation}).%
% src: RELATED-WORK.md#51; paper/zk/data/frozen-dev-stats.json#violation_reason_codes
\
The circuit matters for the opposite verdict and for severity: P1--P3 do not
certify without a matched circuit, and whether a verifier
warning matters to an application depends on what the circuit makes public. Relational
flaws such as Payy's are where joint reasoning would be needed for
detection, and \tool{} does not yet implement them.

\para{A fail-closed architecture is not a sound implementation.} An incorrect key constant caused false certifications in the historical DEV run. It passed 122 controls and 18 soundness
cases because no control used a real default-key verifier. We recommend that
certification-producing analyzers test every positive rule against real
generated artifacts, not only hand-written fixtures.%
% src: paper/zk/data/p4-g2-constant-check.json; reports/soundness/REPORT-2.md (122 controls, 18 cases)

\para{Limitations.}
\emph{Development data.} All validated findings, the pair discrimination
result and the DEV outcomes come from development data; zkDrops is exposed.
Transfer requires the held-out run

\emph{Labels.} Synthetic labels, controls, policies and verifier-kind
classifications were produced with an AI assistant; no human annotation has
yet been completed (\S\ref{sec:ethics}).
\emph{Baselines.} Our arms are the strongest straightforward compositions we
could build, not reproductions of published tools' intended use; ZKAP was
unavailable, and zkFuzz, ELSA and CIVER have not been run.
\emph{Scope.} Circom/Groth16 with snarkjs verifiers and a restricted Solidity
subset; proxies, delegate calls, arbitrary modifiers and inheritance, PLONK
and Noir verifiers produce \UNK{}. P4 is a verifier-configuration check whose
default-key class is a trusted-setup failure outside the core threat model.
\emph{Severity.} Our E4 findings are in hackathon code with no identified
deployment; they demonstrate the mechanism, not financial exposure.%
