\para{Naive composition over-warns and can over-certify.} Arm C flagged all 23
vulnerable cases, 11 of them cases on which \tool{} stayed \UNK{}, but also

Its property harness runs against a mock verifier and provides policy
screening only.

\para{\tool{} trades coverage for restraint.} \tool{} decided 15 of 46 cases:
12 vulnerable cases flagged, 3 legitimate cases flagged, 0 vulnerable cases
certified. It left 20 of 23 legitimate cases \UNK{}, so its 3 false alarms are
a warning rate, not a precision result.%

verifier-arity legitimate cases, which we examine in the frozen rerun
\MISSING{root cause of the 3 synthetic false alarms at the frozen revision}.
The 11 vulnerable families on which \tool{} is \UNK{} are burn ownership,
caller-identity policy, both guard-dominance families, both nullifier-scope
families, replay-position canonicality, decoding order, prefix-versus-full
arrays, overlapping masks and array-length signals.

\subsection{Pair discrimination}\label{sec:res-pairs}

The DEV corpus contains three historical vulnerable/fixed pairs. A tool
discriminates a pair if it warns on the vulnerable revision and not on the
fixed one. Table~\ref{tab:unsafe} shows that neither composed arm
discriminates any pair: on zkDrops, both emit the identical set of finding codes
on the vulnerable and the repaired revision. The frozen \tool{} reports
\VIOL{} on the vulnerable zkDrops revision and \VWS{} on the fixed one; at the
pre-freeze revision it certified both.%
% src: paper/zk/data/pair-discrimination.json#pairs[0] (arm_A/arm_C identical_output true; bindcheck_t25 [VWS,VWS]; bindcheck_frozen [VIOLATION,VWS])
\
The zkDrops pair is exposed---it motivated the position-exact P3 rule---so
this is a development result. Semaphore (the fix is a snarkjs version pin, and
the tracked verifier is a symlink to an untracked build output) and Payy (a
relational defect) remain \UNK{} for every tool.%
