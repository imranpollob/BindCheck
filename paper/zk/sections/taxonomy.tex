\section{A Taxonomy of Binding Flaws}\label{sec:taxonomy}

We group binding flaws by \emph{what the accepted proof fails to fix} and pair
each class with the signature failure it resembles
(Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}). The classes are operational: each corresponds to
a \tool{} rule with a stated positive-evidence condition (\S\ref{sec:design}).
The taxonomy refines the integration layer of the SNARK SoK~\cite{sok-snarks}
rather than replacing it. P3 and P4 are known classes and we credit them as
such. P1 and P2 fall under the SoK's integration design errors without a named
category, and P5 is close to its ``passing unchecked data''.%
% src: RELATED-WORK.md#4-which-p-classes-are-new-by-the-literature-alone
\
The last column of Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} states which artifacts a checker
must read to decide the class. It is the design input for \tool{}, and
Table~\ref{tab:class-census} checks it against what auditors needed.

\para{Effect (P1).}
The consumer commits a transfer, mint, burn or protected state write whose
recipient, amount or identity is neither a public input nor equated to one by
a guard that dominates the effect. The value may also be bound in name only:
Railgun's verifier contract computed the public-input hash from a preimage
that left out two output commitments, so the proof did not fix
them~\cite{abdk-railgun}. \code{msg.sender} is execution context, not proof
authorization. It binds a recipient only if the policy lets any caller
receive the effect.%
% src: reports/R2/findings.csv#R2-RGABDK-CVF-20; docs/verdict-rules.md#recipient_allowed_by_any_caller_policy

\para{Context (P2).}
The proof does not fix the chain, the consumer, the configuration or the kind
of object it speaks about, so a proof valid in one context is accepted in
another. Protocol designs add \code{chainid} and \code{address(this)} to the
public inputs for this reason~\cite{binding-design-2606}. The audits show
subtler contexts: note types inside one pool~\cite{zellic-singularity}, and a
policy selector whose default is an empty policy~\cite{zksecurity-self}.

\para{Uniqueness (P3).}
The replay key is not bound to the proof, not consumed before the effect, or
not canonical modulo the scalar-field order $r$. If the verifier accepts both
$n$ and $n+r$ and the consumer keys a mapping on the raw \code{uint256}, one
proof redeems twice~\cite{zkdrops-pr2,semaphore-16}. A nullifier that
depends on mutable data is the circuit-side variant~\cite{pse-anonaadhaar}.

\para{Verifier and key (P4).}
The verifier accepts too much: a key with $\gamma=\delta$, duplicate input
points, a range check against the base-field modulus $q$ instead of $r$, or a
placeholder that returns \code{true}. A Solidity verifier and its key can be
checked without the circuit or the consumer; a verifier embedded in a
recursive or aggregation circuit cannot, which is why the audit census labels
some P4 findings as needing the circuit. The exploited cases in our census are
concentrated in this class (Table~\ref{tab:class-census}). We treat them as a root-cause
class introduced by generators and templates
(Table~\ref{tab:generators}).

\para{Interface (P5).}
The consumer's public vector disagrees with the verifier's arity or with the
circuit's public signals in count, order or meaning. The order is defined by
the circuit (outputs first, then public inputs in declaration order), so a
contract-only reader cannot decide it~\cite{pse-anonaadhaar}.

\subsection{How verifier interfaces handle field encodings}\label{sec:generators}

The survey follows a public input across the verifier interface. A range
check enforces a unique integer representation. Reduction admits different
integers as the same field element. In a split template the transcript uses
the supplied bytes while field arithmetic uses their residue. The generated
bb.js manual 3.0.0 Keccak templates and the snarkjs 0.7.6 PLONK and FFLONK templates exhibited
that split on source inspection. Later bb Solidity releases in the survey checked the scalar range explicitly. Their same-proof trials rejected the
changed input. Acceptance under a differently constructed proof remains
unconfirmed. The observed reduction occurred in a native verifier. It
establishes no corresponding Solidity or consumer behaviour.

\subsection{Extent binding}\label{sec:extent}

Extent binding is the refinement of P1 in which the unbound effect parameter
is \emph{which elements} of a proved sequence the consumer acts on
(\S\ref{sec:threat}). P1 as usually checked looks at the recipient and amount
of individual effect calls, so it covers neither direction.

\para{(a) Reliance beyond binding.}
The consumer relies on elements the circuit does not constrain. In the Payy
\code{agg\_agg} advisory, the circuit constrained messages only up to an
active count, while the consumer acted on the whole array~\cite{payy-ghsa}.
This direction breaks authorization: an effect depends on an unconstrained
value.

\para{(b) Binding beyond discharge.}
The circuit constrains and applies elements that the consumer never settles.
In Aztec Connect the proof covered every row of a chunk, while the consumer
discharged deposits only below a calldata count
(\S\ref{sec:motivating}). Nothing unauthorized is paid at the moment of
settlement; a required obligation is silently dropped, and the loss comes
later. This direction breaks coverage. An extent rule written for direction
(a) is satisfied by this case, which is why our earlier rule missed it.%
% src: reports/AZ1/REPORT.md#tool--target--verdict (R-EXTENT interface row)

\para{What a checker must know.}
Deciding direction (b) needs four facts: the consumer's loop bound and its
provenance (public input, derived from one, or raw calldata); which elements
the public inputs or a commitment fix, and over what length; whether the
circuit forces elements past the true count to be neutral; and which
per-element effects are required obligations. The first and last are consumer
facts and give a candidate. The third is a circuit fact and decides it: if
padding rows are provably neutral, a short settlement loop is safe. In Aztec
Connect the circuit did force padding to be neutral, but only relative to a
private count that nothing tied to the consumer's count. A consumer-only
repair, requiring every skipped row to be a proved no-op, turns the third fact
into a consumer check; we validate that repair on our reconstruction.%
