Zero-knowledge applications use a proof as a signature: as permission for an
action. An accepting verifier, however, establishes only that the circuit's
relation holds for the public inputs, and the contract that acts on the proof
can act on values the proof never fixed. In June 2026 a zk-rollup contract
lost about \usdM{\AztecLossUSD} this way: the proof covered every row of a
transaction chunk, but the contract settled only a prefix whose length came
from unproven calldata. We call such gaps \emph{binding flaws}. We state the
property they break and give a taxonomy that mirrors classic signature
failures (unsigned fields, missing domain separation, nonce reuse, weak keys,
format mismatch), refined by \emph{extent binding}, which fails in two
directions. In \RtwoAudits{} public audits of ZK protocols,
\RtwoBindingAudits{} report \RtwoBindingFindings{} binding flaws,
\RtwoHighCriticalBinding{} rated High or Critical;
\RtwoBindingNeedsCircuitShare{} cannot be decided from the contract alone. We
present \tool{}, which analyzes the circuit, the generated verifier and the
consuming contract together, fails closed, and checks candidates by
replaying real proofs against unchanged contracts on a local chain.
