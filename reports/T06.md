# T06 — Minimal trace-based coverage checker (observed executions)

Authorized defensive research on self-authored contracts in a local Foundry EVM. This is a **checker for observed executions** of one manually specified property. It is not a static detector, and a clean verdict is not a proof that a contract is safe. The T01 Groth16 setup is a toy with public, recomputable entropy, so none of this speaks to adversarial soundness. Circuit, verifier and all consumers are unchanged.

## 3. Design
- **Observation layer** (`test/T06Export.t.sol`): runs real submissions (low-level call so reverts are captured), reads state before/after through public getters, wraps the call in `vm.startStateDiffRecording` to capture the consumer's actual call to the real verifier (callee, calldata, reverted-in-scope), and adds a clearly labeled direct verifier re-check. It writes `records/exec-NN.json` with decimal-string integers. Records hold **no verdicts, labels, or contract-variant names**. Fields: public inputs and recorded layout, consumer/verifier address, `batchId`, execution id and chain metadata, submission status and revert data, pre/post commitment, per-row funded/amount/consumed, pre/post `consumedBacking` and ETH balance, verifier call evidence.
- **Oracle** (same file, separate section): after hand-written **raw-storage** assertions (`vm.load` of the commitment, backing and escrow slots, plus ETH balance), each scenario writes `oracle/exec-NN.json` with the expected verdict, missing rows/amounts and deficit. The checker never reads that directory; `compare.js` only joins by execution id and compares fields.
- **Manual specification** (`bindcheck/spec/batch_backing.spec.json`, labeled MANUAL): public-input positions, activation semantics, deposit identity `(batchId,row)` with `batchId = keccak256` of the ten 32-byte words, 1 unit = 1 wei, verifier calldata layout. Intended policy written by us, not derived from the circuit or contracts.
- **Checker rules** (all driven by the spec). *Settled:* verifier-call evidence required (selector matches the spec signature, not reverted, and the ten public-input words in its calldata equal the recorded inputs); pre/post commitment must equal the authenticated old/new commitments; every active row needs a funded, exact-amount, previously unconsumed entry that becomes consumed; newly consumed amounts must equal the `consumedBacking` change; each missing obligation is reported with row, amount, reason and whether the escrow was funded-but-unconsumed. Total ETH held is reported but never used as a substitute. Success is taken from the recorded status, not from a commitment change (a legitimate no-op may preserve it). *Reverted:* observed protected state must be identical → "reverted; no committed coverage violation". *Absent required evidence* → `INCONCLUSIVE`; *malformed or inconsistent evidence* → `EVIDENCE_ERROR`.

Checker unit tests (inputs are the real records, perturbed): renaming execution ids/addresses/chain metadata and swapping ids between records leaves verdicts unchanged; source/spec scan finds no variant or scenario names and no hard-coded row/amount literals (regex length quantifiers such as `{40}` excluded); deleting **each** required field (including each row field, a row, and the verifier-call evidence) in every record yields `INCONCLUSIVE`, never a clean verdict; optional fields may be absent; malformed decimals, wrong batchId/layout/selector, verifier-input mismatch, reverted-but-changed state and a flag outside {0,1} yield `EVIDENCE_ERROR`; an unchanged commitment does not hide an unmet obligation. Two tests are labeled **SYNTHETIC** (never executed, not in the table below): a legitimate no-op with old == new commitment is clean, and a replay with entries consumed before the call reports `already_consumed_before_settlement`.

**Example finding (exec-07):** `kind: missing_consumption, row: 3, amount: "40", reason: not_consumed_by_settlement, fundedButUnconsumed: true, escrowAmountHeld: "40"`; accounting: required credit 390, covered 350, newly consumed 350 = `consumedBacking` delta 350, ETH 390 before and after. Note: *a 40-unit consumption shortfall with the 40 wei physically held in unconsumed escrow*, distinct from exec-02 where row 3 was unfunded. In both, the commitment advanced to the authenticated new commitment, so a commitment-only check would call them clean.

**Sensitivity of the comparison (throwaway checker edits, restored, hash and 12/12 verified):** ignoring the post-state consumed flag, and substituting "ETH held ≥ required" for consumption, each make exec-07 come out `NO_VIOLATION`, and `compare.js` reports disagreement (`results/raw/T06/sensitivity/`).

## 6. Manual assumptions and limitations
- The specification is manual intended policy, specific to this fixture; the rules come from it, but the checker was written by the same author as the contracts and oracle, so shared misconceptions are possible. Oracle labels are hand-written per scenario after raw-storage assertions.
- The verifier's boolean return is not captured by the state-diff cheatcode. Evidence is the consumer's observed call and its calldata; the separate `verifierRecheck` is a direct call after the fact and is optional. Acceptance of the proof is inferred from the recorded successful status, which assumes the consumer reverts on a rejected proof.
- Coverage is per observed execution and per observed batch (rows of the recorded `batchId` only); other batches' state, event logs, token flows and cross-transaction behavior are not observed. An exporter fault that rewrites state consistently would be accepted: records are trusted evidence.
- `numRealTxs` is not an input to the checker; it is judged purely on observed consumption. Seven executions, four consumer behaviors, one circuit and one property: no generalization, detection rate or false-positive claim.
- Public inputs and funding amounts are on-chain; commitments are unsalted (no confidentiality). The T02 off-curve gas observation remains an open question and was not investigated. Not run: fresh-machine rehearsal, fuzzing, any external consumer, baseline tools.
