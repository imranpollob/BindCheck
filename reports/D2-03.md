### 3.4 Authorization wording

The audit flags a semantic language error that appears most visibly in the
D1P-43 warning name `effect-authorization` and prose saying a role “authorizes”
an effect. D1P-43's own limitations correctly say that the implementation does
not prove a minimal causal authorization slice, but the stronger label can be
misread.

For paper purposes, a role/amount/beneficiary/domain/state-name match is only
effect-role correspondence at E1. Authorization requires a reviewed permission
record compatible with the committed effect in identity, asset, quantity,
beneficiary, domain, state version, activation condition, and discharge rule.
An accepted proof is E3, not authorization. Silence is not authorization or
safety. The historical report and implementation were not renamed in this
task; the claim ledger supplies the safe interpretation.

## 4. Closest primary related work

### ZKAP

The [USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity24-wen_1.pdf)
and [artifact](https://github.com/whbjzzwjxq/ZKAP) were accessible. ZKAP's CDG,
nine detectors, and explicit unused-public-input detector overlap producer-side
R-BIND. The inspected paper does not extract a Solidity consumer, but that does
not prove an extension is impossible. The minimal test fixes one circuit and
varies only whether two consumers commit a protected effect from the same
unbound word.

### CIVER

The [IEEE publisher record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10646792/)
and [artifact](https://github.com/costa-group/circom_civer) were inspected; the
full publisher paper was not retrieved. CIVER overlaps modular verification,
pre/postconditions, tags, and rich circuit properties. Its current artifact is
Circom-focused and lists ACIR as upcoming, but native incompatibility is not a
method failure. The minimal comparison gives CIVER an equivalent stated
property and counts every annotation and adapter.

### CCC-Check

The [author-hosted paper](https://vdb.space/papers/zkp2026.pdf) and
[artifact](https://github.com/ArmanKolozyan/CCC-Check) were accessible.
CCC-Check relates prover computation to constraints through CirC IR; its paper
states that CirC frontends include Noir, Circom, and ZoKrates. This can overlap
producer defects beyond occurrence counting. The smallest distinguishing design
uses one producer computation/constraint mutant and one consumer-only mutant
with byte-identical producer artifacts.

### SmartAxe

The [author-hosted paper](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~juanzhai/papers/fse24liao.pdf)
and [artifact index](https://github.com/InPlusLab/FSE24-SmartAxe) were
accessible. SmartAxe is a close structural analogue: it joins control/data-flow
facts across bridge contracts, infers protected resources, and detects semantic
inconsistency. It does not consume ZK constraints in the inspected paper, but
cross-program semantic joining is not new. A fair comparison needs either a
native paired bridge or a strongest simple graph-composition adaptation; a
same-chain non-pair is unsupported, not missed.

### ELSA

## 6. Smallest prospective decision experiment

The common experiment shape is:

1. freeze a producer and mapping;
2. create paired consumers that change only the protected effect's relevance or
   the state/domain/beneficiary relationship;
3. add semantics-preserving identifier renamings and dead-code perturbations;
4. compare producer-only, consumer-only, unjoined union, strongest simple
   composition, full BindCheck, and applicable closest/same-policy arms;
5. equalize facts, specifications, budgets, and validation opportunity;
6. charge annotations, adapters, prompts/models, authoring, correction,
   execution, proving, and adjudication;
7. report unsupported and unresolved outcomes without treating them as misses;
   and
8. validate every candidate through the same E1-to-E4 procedure.

An E1 difference on exposed cases can motivate later work. It cannot by itself
establish the proposed contribution. If a simple composition or closest tool
matches the relationship diagnostics, narrow the contribution. If automated E4
or supported held-out transfer is absent, the capability gate remains open.
