Zero-knowledge applications use a proof as a signature: as permission for an action. An accepting verifier, however, establishes only that the circuit's relation holds for the public inputs, and the contract that acts on the proof can act on values the proof never fixed. In June 2026 a zk-rollup contract lost about $2.1M this way: the proof covered every row of a transaction chunk, but the contract settled only a prefix whose length came from unproven calldata. We call such gaps binding flaws. We state the property they break and give a taxonomy that mirrors classic signature failures (unsigned fields, missing domain separation, nonce reuse, weak keys, format mismatch), refined by extent binding, which fails in two directions. In 68 public audits of ZK protocols, 18 report 34 binding flaws, 17 rated High or Critical; 38.2% cannot be decided from the contract alone. We present BindCheck, which analyzes the circuit, the generated verifier and the consuming contract together, fails closed, and checks candidates by replaying real proofs against unchanged contracts on a local chain.
