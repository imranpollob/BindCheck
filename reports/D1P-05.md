## 3. macOS resource-policy replacement

### 3.1 Mechanism

The D1P-04 `RLIMIT_AS` hook was removed only from the new D1P-05 runner. The
new runner uses `/usr/lib/libproc.dylib`:

- `proc_listchildpids` recursively enumerates the solver root and descendants;
- `proc_pidinfo(PROC_PIDTASKINFO)` reads each observed process's resident
  bytes; and
- every sample sums resident bytes over the observed process tree.

Production sampling is every **0.05 seconds**. An observed aggregate strictly
above **4,294,967,296 bytes** triggers `SIGKILL` for the solver process group.
The unchanged 30-second deadline also terminates the process group. Monitoring
or launch failure stops the campaign and automatically produces a structured
ledger; a cell timeout, solver UNKNOWN, or observed memory cutoff leaves that
cell unusable while the fixed schedule continues.

This policy is **not a hard address-space cap**. RSS can grow between samples
and overshoot 4 GiB before termination. A sufficiently short-lived allocation
or descendant can exist between observations. Consequently these measurements
are sampled observations, not allocation bounds and not timing/resource parity
with the historical Linux `RLIMIT_AS` campaigns.

Every solver call received at least one sample. The campaign's maximum observed
process-tree RSS was **9,322,496 bytes**, on the known-equivalence control.
Each cell completed in at most 0.061192 seconds and received one sample; their
small reported peaks (maximum 704,512 bytes) are early observed samples, not
claims about unobserved between-sample maxima.
