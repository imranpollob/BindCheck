## Method

- **Denominator unit.** One in-scope report is one unique `report_url` with `screen1 = IN` in `audit-sweep/merged/all_reports.csv`. A URL that one agent marked IN and another marked OUT counts as IN. All 255 labeled findings (duplicates and U removed) map to these reports.
- **Primary denominator.** It excludes the 11 IN reports whose finding text was never public: 6 Cyfrin summary-only PDFs and 5 Cantina competitions. These reports cannot contribute a numerator. The sensitivity table keeps them.
- **Year.** Each report gets one year: the first 2015–2029 year in the sweep's `report_date`, or else the year in the labels' `report_date`. Titles and URLs are not parsed. Every labeled finding inherits its report's year. The per-bin counts reproduce CHARACTERIZATION.md exactly.
- **Metrics:**
  - **A (primary):** the share of reports with at least one binding finding. This is binomial, with a Wilson 95% CI.
  - **B:** binding findings per report. Findings cluster within reports, so the CI comes from a seeded cluster bootstrap, not Wilson. The table also shows the largest contribution from any one report.
  - **C:** binding findings divided by labeled findings, with a Wilson CI. Labeled findings are screened candidates (KEEP/BORDERLINE), not every finding in a ZK report. C therefore measures how much of the screened pool is binding, not the binding share of all ZK findings.
- **Pre-specified comparison.** The comparison covers 2023–2025, the only years with roughly 60 or more reports each. 2021–2022 are pooled (12 reports). 2026 is a partial year. The trend test is Cochran-Armitage over 2023, 2024 and 2025.
- **Sets.** Everything is run for all 103 binding findings and for the 87 core findings (PSS = Y excluded).

## Does the rising trend survive normalization?

**Only in direction, not statistically.**

- **Raw counts.** Binding findings triple from 2023 to 2025 (13 to 39), while in-scope reports rise by about 1.6× (60 to 94).
- **After normalization:**
  - The share of reports with at least one binding finding goes from 0.17 [0.09, 0.28] in 2023 to 0.27 [0.19, 0.36] in 2025. 2024 is flat at 0.17. The CIs overlap, and the Cochran-Armitage trend test gives p = 0.12 (z = 1.56).
  - Binding findings per report go from 0.22 to 0.41, and the bootstrap CIs overlap.
  - The binding share of labeled findings goes from 0.34 to 0.48 (p = 0.18).
- **Core 87.** The trend is weaker: report share 0.15 → 0.22, p = 0.22.
- **Undated reports.** Allocating all 61 undated reports to either end of the window does not reach p < 0.05 (p from 0.07 to 0.28).
- **Conclusion.** Growth in the number of published ZK reports explains roughly 40% of the raw increase on a log scale (1.6× of 3×). The remainder is a non-significant upward tendency.
- **Correction to the paper.** The `\DEMO` figure in `study.tex` ("rises from 0.21 to 0.34 binding findings per report") is a placeholder and does not match these data. The measured per-report rate is 0.22 (2023) → 0.41 (2025), and it is not significant. The sentence should not claim a rising rate.

## Limitations

- **Undated reports.** 61 of the 311 primary-denominator reports (20%) are undated, against 16 of 103 binding findings. They are concentrated in one source: 41 of the 61 are ABDK, whose dates were not parsed. The missingness is therefore differential by source, which is why the adversarial-allocation bound is reported.
- **Cluster effect in 2024.** One 2024 report contributes 8 binding findings. This inflates metric B for 2024; metric A is robust to it.
- **Sweep coverage.** Coverage gaps (CertiK, Solodit, Oxorio, most of yAcademy; see COVERAGE.md) are constant across years only if those sources' year mix matches the swept sources. This is not checked.
- **Dates.** Nethermind dates are "first date in text", which may be the audit start rather than the publication date.
- **Unique-report count.** It is 322 by URL, versus FUNNEL.md's 343, which dedups on a different key. The 343 is not reused here.

## Paper-ready sentences (for §4, Trend)

Raw counts of binding findings triple from 2023 to 2025 (13 to 39), but the number of in-scope ZK audit reports also grows, from 60 to 94. Normalized by those reports, the share of reports with at least one binding finding moves from 0.17 (95% Wilson CI 0.09–0.28) to 0.27 (0.19–0.36), and binding findings per report move from 0.22 to 0.41. The change is not statistically significant (Cochran-Armitage over 2023–2025, p = 0.12; core-only p = 0.22), so our data cannot distinguish a rising rate from a flat one. Part of the raw growth tracks the growth in published ZK audits.
