**SoK statement on defenses.** §8 says "there are currently no specific defenses developed for vulnerabilities in the integration layer of SNARK systems." The Table 7 caption says no defense detects bugs in that layer.

## 1. Mapping rule (written before mapping)

**Assignment.** Each binding item gets exactly one SoK category: the one the SoK's own definitions and worked examples would most plausibly assign, judged by where the faulty logic lives (the SoK classifies by layer). The D3 layer tag is the starting point:

- CONTRACT-ONLY or BOTH → integration (V4–V7);
- CIRCUIT-ONLY → circuit (V1–V3);
- VERIFIER/OFFCHAIN → backend (V12, V10, V11) or circuit V1 for under-constrained aggregation/recursion circuits.

The SoK's worked examples override the layer tag:

- nullifier range check → V4;
- nullifier duplication or management → V7;
- an aggregation/recursion circuit that is under-constrained → V1.

**Fit grade.** Each assignment also gets a fit grade:

- **DIRECT.** The SoK definition describes the binding mechanism itself. Examples: a public input the consumer fails to validate (V4), an under-constrained output (V1), a verifier missing a check (V12).
- **LAYER-ONLY.** The category is reached only through layer placement or the catch-all wording of V7 ("logic that operates in conjunction with ZKPs"). The defining fact of the binding flaw is that the effect value or context was never in the statement, and no SoK definition names that. Examples: a payout to `msg.sender`, chain ID absent from the statement, caller arguments the proof never covers.
- **NONE.** No SoK category applies, even by layer.

**Reported quantities:**
- how many of the 103 (and 87 core) fall in the integration layer, other layers, or no category;
- the same counts restricted to DIRECT fits;
- which of P1–P5 the SoK has no DIRECT category for (a pattern counts as uncovered if fewer than half of its items map DIRECT).

## 4. Reading the results

- **Integration layer vs other layers.** 49 of 103 binding findings (48%) fall in the SoK's integration layer; for the core 87 it is 49 (56%). Only 28 of those 49 fit an integration definition DIRECTLY:
  - V4 Passing Unchecked Data: 15;
  - V7 nullifier/replay management and verification-skipping logic: 9;
  - V6 composition: 3;
  - V5 delegation: 1.

  The remaining findings fall in other layers:
  - circuit (V1 under-constrained): 30, of which 23 are DIRECT;
  - backend (V12 Unsafe Verifier): 22, of which 21 are DIRECT;
  - no SoK category: 2 (L003, a parser discrepancy in an off-chain library; L194, interchangeable proofs across two circuits).
- **Patterns without a SoK category:**
  - **P1 has no SoK category.** Only 8 of 23 map DIRECTLY. 14 of 23 reach V7 only through its catch-all wording ("logic that operates in conjunction with ZKPs"): payouts to `msg.sender`, caller-chosen fees, pools and recipients, values the contract fills in after verification. The SoK's V4 is about public inputs the consumer fails to *validate*, while P1 is about effect values that were never public inputs.
  - **P2 is partly uncovered.** 10 of 23 are not DIRECT, mostly chain, domain or configuration context absent from the statement (L057, L074, L100, L149, L172, L194).
  - **P3, P4 and P5 are covered.** P3 maps to V4 or V7 (nullifier range and management) and V1. P4 maps to V12 and V1 for verifier and recursion bugs, plus V6 for composition. P5 maps to V4.
- **Count comparison.** The SoK reports 13 integration-layer bugs out of 141. This dataset has 49 binding findings that the SoK's own scheme would place in the integration layer (28 by a direct definition fit), about 3.8× (2.2× direct). RELWORK-SCAN.md's "about 8x" compares all 103 binding findings to the SoK's 13. That figure counts circuit and backend findings that the SoK itself would place outside the integration layer, so the paper should use the like-for-like figure.
- **Limitation.** The mapping depends on the D3 layer tags, which are also still pending your check. The DIRECT vs LAYER-ONLY grade is a judgment; 21 of the 49 integration assignments are LAYER-ONLY.

## 5. Paper-ready sentences (for §8 Related Work / §4; AI-proposed mapping pending author check)

Mapped onto the vulnerability taxonomy of Chaliasos et al., 49 of our 103 binding findings fall in their integration layer, compared with the 13 integration bugs in their dataset. The remaining 54 fall in their circuit layer (30, under-constrained), among unsafe-verifier bugs (22), or in no category (2). Only 28 of the 49 match an integration definition directly, and the SoK has no category for our P1: 14 of 23 unbound-recipient and unbound-amount findings reach it only through the catch-all "ZKP complementary logic", because the effect value was never a public input that could be checked.
